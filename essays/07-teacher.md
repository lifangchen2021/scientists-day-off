# 老師

我一直是那種很叛逆的學生，所以即便知道自己有天份，我也不敢當老師，因為我怕有一天，報應會找上門。

當然以上都是玩笑話，我應該只能算是「比較喜歡胡思亂想」的學生吧，所以我從國高中開始，就深度思考了各種「如果我是老師」會遇到的可能情境，但從各種沙盤推演結果來看，說我會有101種死法都不為過。

我想大家都承認老師這個行業在社會上的地位很崇高，至少我們那個年代是這樣，學生們都喜歡老師；父母都希望自己的兒子找個老師女朋友或媳婦，但即便在那樣的時代背景下，我都深深嗅出了老師是「高風險職業」的味道。別笑，我是認真的，以我極端敏感的共情能力和稍帶江湖義氣的性格，我想我只要遇到一兩個需要幫助的學生，我就足以把自己拖入地獄；就算我沒有把自拖入地獄裡，也難保社會輿論不會把我釘在恥辱柱上，人言可畏。

當然實際狀況也沒有「小時候的我」想像的那麼糟，教育學院有一系列的課程，教導怎樣當一位合格的老師，以及老師應該如何保護自己，無奈我就像是小時候被綁過鐵球的大象，因為「思想鋼印」作祟，再也沒有辦法提起勇氣選擇這行業。

人們總喜歡看「春風化雨」這類的故事，期待自己遇到足以改變人生軌跡的老師，幻想老師和學生互相成就的感人情節。但很少人想過，這樣的老師究竟要承擔多少心理壓力、輿論風險，甚至還有可能惹上法律的麻煩。而現在這個年代相較於以前，老師們的立場與工作環境，似乎又更艱難了…。

這讓現在的我在面對「老師」時，有著五味雜陳的情感，還稍稍有點抬不起頭來的意味，因為我知道自己其實是個「逃兵」，老師們做的，是我當年有興趣想做，但沒膽子做的行業。

隨著年紀越長，人是會越來越膽小的，我想我現在的膽量，大概小到但凡這社會上有人做的，是我不敢、不願意、沒能力做的事，我都會給予深深的尊重。

## English — Teacher

I’ve always been the kind of student who’s rebellious by nature. So even though I knew I had some talent, I never dared to become a teacher—because I was afraid that one day, karma would come for me.

Of course, that’s just a joke. I guess I was more of a student who liked to overthink things. Ever since middle and high school, I’ve spent a lot of time imagining what might happen if I were a teacher. And judging from all those mental simulations, it wouldn’t be an exaggeration to say there would be at least 101 ways for me to die.

I think everyone would agree that teaching is a highly respected profession—at least it was in my generation. Students admired their teachers; parents hoped their sons would marry a teacher. But even in that kind of social climate, I could still sense that being a teacher was a “high-risk profession.” Don’t laugh—I’m serious. With my overly sensitive empathy and my somewhat chivalrous sense of justice, I knew that if I ever met one or two students who needed help, I’d be doomed to drag myself into hell with them. And even if I didn’t, there’s no guarantee that public opinion wouldn’t crucify me anyway—people’s words can be terrifying.

Of course, reality isn’t quite as grim as my younger self imagined. In teacher education, there are plenty of courses that train you to be a competent teacher and teach you how to protect yourself. But I guess I’m like an elephant once chained to a heavy ball—scarred by the “iron imprint” of my own thoughts. No matter how much I learn, I can’t summon the courage to enter this profession.

People love stories about “teachers who change lives”—the kind that make us hope to meet someone who could alter our destiny, the kind that celebrate mutual inspiration between teacher and student. But few ever stop to think how much psychological burden, social pressure, and even legal risk such teachers have to bear. And in this day and age, compared to before, it seems that teachers’ roles and working environments have only grown more difficult.

So now, whenever I face a teacher, I’m filled with a mix of emotions—and maybe even a little shame. Because deep down, I know I’m a deserter. Teachers are doing the very work I once dreamed of doing but never had the courage to pursue.

As people grow older, they also grow more timid. I suppose I’ve reached the point where anyone doing something I can’t, won’t, or dare not do automatically earns my deepest respect.
