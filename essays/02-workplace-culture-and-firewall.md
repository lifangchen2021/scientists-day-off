# 職場文化與防火牆

![職場文化與防火牆插圖](../assets/workplace-culture-and-firewall.jpg)

我不是很確定要怎要看待導師制度，我當然明白這是一種教育傳承的概念，但可能是因為我本身是在職生，在面對這個制度的時候，疑惑感很重，在我的觀念裡，在職生就是要自己包辦掉所有的任務，因為工作上就是如此，應該盡量不給別人帶來麻煩。現在的我大概對於理解學校的運作邏輯有些困難，畢竟江湖險惡。

在學校學生總是會畢業，所以學長可能還會願意教學弟妹，但職場往往是一輩子的事，資源爭奪的狀況會比較明顯，很難冀望有人會教你什麼，或提供資源給你。比如某次工作需要，我必須要用XRD，結果在我自己的材料所竟然借不到，還被儀器管理人破口大罵掛電話，最後我是到化學所才借到儀器，工作業務報告也是，會莫名其妙地被指責都在亂說之類(但我必須承認拜這些人的言論所賜，我只好自己投稿SoftwareX證明自己──如果環境不支持，那就往外去尋)。雖然乍看之下覺得不可思議，但是把資源有限的觀點帶入，就很好理解；反正工作的事都不是私人的事，有時限當下我會想辦法解決，但絕對不會有下一次，所以實驗工作我之後很不客氣地推掉了。職場上的合作樣態更是糟糕，在我眼裡幾乎到了「黑吃黑」的地步，就是在比誰比較好騙而已，所以我很習慣單兵作業，但這大概不是什麼值得驕傲的事就是了；我不是不喜歡合作，但會慎選合作對象。

我會猶豫應該和學生聊這些嗎？天真在職場上可不是什麼會被推崇的美德，我無意評論個人應該如何選擇，因為每個人能承擔的風險和損失都不同，但我仍舊認為學生可以思考以下這件事：在工作上，你能接受的影響邊界在哪裡？簡單說就是設立防火牆的概念，若要我建議，大概是如果這份工作或某個人，影響到你對「人事物(包含你自己)」的價值觀，那可能需要提高警覺。笛卡兒的「我思故我在」，其真實意涵遠不像字面那樣優雅輕巧；在職場，批判思考和自我觀照是護盾，可以保證你的人格不受侵害。

## English — Workplace Culture and Firewalls

I’m not sure how to view the mentoring system. I understand it as a form of educational inheritance, but as a working student, I often feel uncertain about it. In my world, you handle things on your own and avoid troubling others — that’s how the workplace works. Perhaps that’s why I find it hard to grasp how schools operate; the real world is far less forgiving.

In school, seniors may help juniors, but in the workplace — a lifelong arena — competition for resources is sharper, and few are willing to share. Once I needed to use an XRD but couldn’t borrow one from my own lab. The manager yelled and hung up on me; I eventually borrowed one from another department. Similar things happen with reports — unjust criticism is common. In the end, I published in SoftwareX just to prove my competence. If the environment doesn’t support you, seek another one.

At first, these experiences seemed absurd, but once you recognize that resources are limited, they make sense. Work, after all, isn’t personal — it’s bound by deadlines. I’ll find ways to handle things in the moment, but I never let the same problem happen twice. That’s why I later refused to take on any more lab work. Collaboration in the workplace can be even worse — almost a “dog-eat-dog” situation, where it’s a contest of who can outsmart or exploit whom. So I’ve gotten used to working alone, though that’s nothing to be proud of. It’s not that I dislike collaboration; I just choose my collaborators carefully.

Should I share this with students? Naivety isn’t a virtue at work. Everyone bears different risks, but I think students should ask themselves: Where do I draw the line of what I can accept? In other words, build your own firewall.

If a job or a person starts to distort your values — your sense of people, things, or yourself — be cautious. Descartes’s “I think, therefore I am” isn’t as elegant as it sounds. In the real world, critical thinking and self-reflection are shields that protect your integrity.
